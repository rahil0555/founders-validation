# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain is real for all three parties.** Students have learning gaps, parents want to see progress, and schools want results they can show. The dream outcome (a tutor that adapts to each child) is easy to understand and worth a lot.
- **Schools are easy to find and reach.** A mapped list of 150 schools across four zones, each with a named owner, is the reachability the market test asks for. One signed school brings a group of students, not one.
- **There is a working product.** Diagnostics, adaptive flows, narration and parent visibility already exist. That means the offer can be tested now, not after a build.
- **Parent visibility can lift likelihood.** If progress tracking shows a parent proof they can see, it raises perceived likelihood. That is the weakest variable in most offers.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is close to zero today, for every party.** There are no pilots, no measured outcomes and no users. A school is asked to believe an AI platform will close gaps with no evidence. A parent is asked to pay ₹299 on the school's word. Without proof the value equation falls apart, and nothing else in the offer makes up for it.
2. **The effort and sacrifice fall on the school, and the payer is unclear.** Educator orientation, student onboarding, baseline diagnostics and progress reviews all cost school staff time. The person who carries that effort (the school) may not be the payer (the parent), and the parent never chose the product. If no one party gets enough outcome to justify its effort or spend, the deal stalls between them. The revenue share with schools is still unknown, so we cannot tell whether the school has a reason to push it.
3. **The offer is a feature list, not an outcome, so it will slide into price competition.** "Adaptive learning, AI explanations, TTS, tracking" describes what the product does, not the result the buyer gets. Without a named, measurable outcome, a time frame and a guarantee, schools and parents will compare it on price against other learning apps and tuition. ₹299 with no billing period stated is not yet a price anyone can judge. Who else is in this market is unknown (/founder-competitors).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who is the buyer: the school, the parent, or both? Who signs, and who pays?
- What single outcome can you promise, and by when? For example, a measured gain on the baseline diagnostic within one term.
- What guarantee could you offer a pilot school (free until a target is hit, or money back) without breaking the economics?
- Is ₹299 per month, per term or per year? Have any parents been asked to pay it? (/founder-pricing, /founder-consumer)
- What is the AI/API cost per active student, and what is left after the school's share? (/founder-cfo)
- What does the school get out of this: results to show parents, less teacher workload, or a revenue share? How much staff time does a pilot take?
- Could you double the price and still be the obvious choice? If not, what would have to be added to the stack?
- What would a parent see in week one that makes them believe it is working?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove likelihood cheaply first.** Run 2-3 low-cost pilots where the baseline diagnostic is re-run at the end of the term, and report the change plainly, good or bad.
2. **Rewrite the offer around one outcome with a guarantee.** Name the result, the time frame and the risk reversal for the school (for example, a pilot is free unless an agreed gain is reached).
3. **Settle who pays and test it.** Before scaling outreach, confirm the payer, the billing period and the school's share, and get real willingness-to-pay evidence from parents in the pilot schools.
4. **Cut the school's effort.** Make onboarding something the team delivers, not the teachers, so effort no longer outweighs the value for the channel.
5. **Show the unit economics.** Revenue per paid student after the school's share must cover the AI cost per student, with that cost measured in the pilots, not assumed.
